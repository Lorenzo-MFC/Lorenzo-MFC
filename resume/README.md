# Resume: Business Operations Manager

The resume is in [`business-operations-manager-resume.md`](business-operations-manager-resume.md).
It is a **structured draft**. Replace every `[placeholder]` with real, checkable data before
you send it. Recruiters ask about the numbers in interviews, so only use figures you can back up.

## How to fill in the numbers

A Business Operations Manager is hired to improve **processes**, **economics** and
**people management**. Every bullet should show one of these with a number:

| What to quantify | Where to find it (fitness club) | Example bullet ending |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue & growth | Annual accounts, POS / management software | "...grew revenue from €X to €Y (+Z%)" |
| Costs | Supplier invoices, utility bills, contracts | "...cut operating costs by X%" |
| Retention / churn | Membership renewals ÷ expiring memberships | "...renewal rate from X% to Y%" |
| Unit economics | Marketing spend ÷ new members = CAC; avg. monthly fee × avg. months = LTV | "...LTV/CAC ratio of X:1" |
| Efficiency | Staff hours, class occupancy, waiting times | "...saved X hours/week" |
| Team | Headcount managed, turnover | "...led a team of N" |

Bullet formula: **action verb + what you did + measurable result**.

## Tailoring to each job posting (ATS)

1. Copy the posting's key terms into the *Core Competencies* table and the summary when
   they are true for you. Common ones: *process improvement, KPI, budgeting, forecasting,
   cross-functional, stakeholder management, SOP, P&L, vendor management, OKR*.
2. Keep the resume to **1 page** (2 at most) and export it to PDF.
3. Put your strongest 3 bullets first under each role.
4. Before sending, delete the placeholder note at the top of the resume and any line
   that doesn't apply (e.g. ASD/SSD if the club is not a sports association).
